REAP Books

Local-first bookkeeping for small US agencies and independents. It turns a folder of receipts and a bank statement into invoices, a clean expense ledger, and a reconciled month — running on your own machine, against your own files and your own credentials.

Pre-release. There is no install to recommend yet. The source is public and the work is in the open.

How it works

REAP is an MCP server. You point an AI assistant at a folder on your machine, and then you talk to it: set up your business, invoice a client, work through a month of receipts, reconcile against your bank export. 

reap init --workspace ~/my-books
claude mcp add reap -- reap serve --workspace ~/my-books

Three things it will not do

  • It will not let a model do your arithmetic. A language model doing sums produces confident wrong answers, which in bookkeeping means someone files wrong numbers. Every figure REAP asserts is computed from integer cents by ordinary code, and is reproducible by running that code over the same inputs. The model reads receipts, suggests categories, and drafts prose.
  • It will not send your tax ID or bank details to a model. They stay in a file on your machine. The model-facing surface receives a redacted view — everything else, with those two replaced by a flag saying they exist. This is enforced by tests that run on every change, not by anyone remembering.
  • It will not phone home. There is no REAP server. No account, no signup, no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting. A finance tool that phones home is one nobody should install, and there is a test that fails if a stray URL appears in the source.

Where your data lives

  • On your machine. Your business details, your receipts, your bank statements, and the invoices REAP generates.
  • In your own Google account, if you choose to connect one. REAP creates one spreadsheet — your ledger — and can touch nothing else in your Drive.

Nowhere else. See the privacy policy, which is short.

Open source

Apache-2.0. The source, including the tests that back up the claims above, is at github.com/talktalkmake/reapbooks — private while REAP is pre-release, public at launch.
