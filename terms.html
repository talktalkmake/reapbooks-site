It is a tool, not an accountant

REAP helps you keep records. It does not give tax, accounting, financial or legal advice, and nothing it produces is a professional opinion.

It is deliberately built to avoid overstepping that line. It records what you spent and groups expenses the way a Schedule C asks for them, but it computes no deduction — the deductible share of a meal, a phone bill or a vehicle depends on circumstances the software cannot see, and that judgment belongs to a qualified person. It prints a late-fee policy if you give it one; it never calculates a late fee. It checks that a bank routing number is well formed; it cannot tell you the account exists or belongs to who you think.

You remain responsible for your own books, your own filings and your own invoices. Check the figures before you send them.
